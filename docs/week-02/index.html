Why integrate the forecast?

The original workbook projected THX-101 sales from management assumptions that we invented. Those assumptions can be useful for planning, but they are not an independently tested forecasting method. A more useful Week 2 comparison puts two statistical forecasts on equal footing against the same historical data.

I removed the old THX-101 annual-growth forecast and research-client-growth formulas rather than simply hiding the management option. The Forecast Dashboard now separates historical smoothing from forward forecasting for each revenue stream. THX-101 has one optional historical smoothing selector in B5 (None, SMA 3M/6M/12M, or EMA 3M), and separate ARIMA A/B forecasts in B6/D6. Research services has one optional historical smoothing selector in B16 and ARIMA A/B forecasts in B17/D17. The selected moving average appears as a single dotted historical chart overlay or disappears entirely with None. Historical moving averages never select the operating forecast: ARIMA forecast A drives the linked revenue and financial statements; ARIMA B is a comparison only. The financial operating schedule stays in Revenue & Demand, while calculations, backtests, diagnostics, and the chart source data reside in Forecast Analysis.

Data and evaluation

I used 33 months each of synthetic THX-101 and research-services sales from January 2024 through September 2026. Each method was tested using a fixed forecast origin: the first 27 months (through March 2026) were used for training, while April–September 2026 was withheld for a six-month out-of-sample comparison. The forecast horizon runs from October 2026 through September 2027.

For a fair comparison, I evaluated all methods against the same six withheld observations using mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) and root mean squared error (RMSE). MAPE gives proportional error; RMSE expresses error in dollars and penalizes larger misses. All six holdout months are forecast from a single March 2026 origin. Moving averages recurse on their own predictions after that date, exactly as they do for the 12-month forward scenario. An earlier version mistakenly kept moving-average holdout predictions constant, overstating their measured errors.

Model selection: results differ by revenue stream

In the fixed six-month holdout, ARIMA with drift has the lowest THX-101 error (3.15%). For research services, the simple EMA benchmark was lower (5.89%) than either ARIMA candidate; among the ARIMA-family forecasts, the seasonal approximation performed better (11.21%) than nonseasonal drift (13.98%). The dashboard keeps SMA/EMA for historical smoothing and lets the user compare these two ARIMA forecasts independently. The lower EMA benchmark is disclosed, not concealed.

ARIMA(0,1,0) with drift recorded the lowest six-month holdout error, 3.15% MAPE (RMSE $0.344 million). Its advantage makes sense: monthly sales increased steadily, so a model that extends the average historical monthly change tracks this dataset better than a moving average that lags behind the rising level.

The twelve-month moving average performed particularly poorly on THX-101 (27.35% MAPE) because it averages much lower historical sales. But the same selection rule is not optimal for every part of the business. On research-services revenue, EMA 3M has the lowest tested error at 5.89% MAPE, while ARIMA with drift records 13.98%. This is why the two streams have independent historical smoothing controls and separate ARIMA forecast controls.

Stationarity and seasonality

The level series trends upward. An earlier Augmented Dickey–Fuller diagnostic returned a level-series p-value near 0.945; after first differencing, the p-value was approximately 0.057, still slightly above the conventional 5% threshold. I therefore treated first differencing as a practical specification and reviewed ACF/PACF diagnostics on the monthly changes.

For THX-101, the differenced series had a lag-12 correlation near zero. Its seasonal model achieved 3.58% MAPE, close to but higher than the 3.15% nonseasonal result. Research-services seasonality was less stable: the unconstrained seasonal autoregression coefficient exceeded 1, causing implausible long-horizon growth. I capped that coefficient at ±0.90 for a provisional comparison; even then it did not beat EMA (research seasonal MAPE: 11.21%). These are short synthetic series, not strong evidence of stable annual seasonality.

Business interpretation and limitations

In one example comparison for September 2027, ARIMA with drift projects $13,257,197 in THX-101 monthly sales and seasonal ARIMA projects $13,322,605. Selecting different methods allows us to quantify changes in demand, manufacturing requirements, cash flow, and future financing needs.

I tested each independent research selector: changing research Projection B altered only the comparison, while changing research Projection A changed research revenue and retained zero balance-sheet and cash-flow reconciliation differences in the checked periods. I performed the corresponding link test for THX-101 as well. Historical synthetic observations stay unchanged.

The course comparison emphasizes a 12-month forecasting horizon (October 2026–September 2027). The workbook extends the statistical projections through December 2032 to keep the existing three-statement model connected, but those later years are illustrative scenario sensitivities, not validated multi-year predictions.

The main limitation is that the history itself is synthetic and follows assumptions we originally designed. ARIMA's 3.15% is a six-month holdout MAPE on that constructed series—not a claim of 96.85% real-world accuracy, nor a validated error rate for future sales. Regulatory outcomes, reimbursement, supply constraints, and competition can change revenue in ways that an extrapolation cannot predict.

Implementation note: the workbook uses spreadsheet formulas, not VBA. Moving averages, EMA, and ARIMA(0,1,0) with drift recalculate from each revenue stream's history. The seasonal alternatives use a conditional lag-12 approximation, not a full maximum-likelihood SARIMA fit; the research-series coefficient is bounded for stability. THX-101 ADF p-values are archived diagnostic snapshots, not live recalculations. Clinical approvals and financing schedules remain business scenarios because no suitable realized history exists for a statistical sales forecast.
